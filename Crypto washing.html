// 01 — CORE STAGES

The three-stage
laundering model

Every money laundering operation — crypto or fiat — follows the same fundamental architecture. Here's how each stage works on-chain.

01
💰
PLACEMENT

Entering the system

Criminal proceeds are converted into cryptocurrency to enter the formal financial system. This is the highest-risk stage — the moment of greatest exposure.

P2P exchanges with no KYC requirements
Cryptocurrency ATMs in unregulated jurisdictions
Corrupt money service businesses (MSBs)
Structuring: breaking large amounts into small buys
Gift cards → crypto conversion chains
02
🔀
LAYERING

Obscuring the trail

A complex web of transactions is created to distance funds from their source. Blockchain forensics tools target this stage — it leaves the most data.

Chain-hopping: moving funds across multiple blockchains
Mixers & tumblers: pooling and redistributing funds
Privacy coins: Monero, Zcash conversion
DeFi protocols: automated, pseudonymous swaps
Nested exchange accounts and shell wallets
03
✅
INTEGRATION

Re-entering legitimacy

Funds re-enter the legitimate economy with a plausible cover story. Once integrated, tracing the origin becomes extremely difficult.

Real estate purchases via crypto-friendly jurisdictions
Luxury goods, NFTs and digital asset flipping
Shell company invoicing and trade manipulation
OTC broker withdrawals to clean bank accounts
Crypto lending platforms as liquidity laundering

// 03 — OBFUSCATION TECHNIQUES

Common laundering
techniques

Each technique exploits specific properties of blockchain technology to reduce traceability.

🌪
Crypto Mixers / Tumblers

Pools funds from multiple users and redistributes equivalent amounts to break on-chain links. Tornado Cash processed $7B+ before OFAC sanctions in 2022.

DETECTION: HIGH
🔗
Chain Hopping

Moving assets across multiple blockchains (BTC → ETH → BSC → Solana) using cross-chain bridges. Each hop fragments the forensic trail across different ledgers.

DETECTION: MEDIUM
🕵️
Privacy Coins

Monero (XMR) uses ring signatures, stealth addresses, and RingCT to make transactions untraceable by design. Zcash shielded pools offer similar properties.

DETECTION: VERY LOW
🏦
DeFi Layering

Using Uniswap, Curve, Aave and other protocols for automated swaps. Smart contracts execute without human intermediaries, making intervention points scarce.

DETECTION: MEDIUM
🎨
NFT Wash Trading

Selling NFTs between self-controlled wallets at inflated prices creates artificial "sale records." The buyer's payment appears as legitimate art investment income.

DETECTION: HIGH
🎰
Crypto Gambling Sites

Depositing dirty crypto, gambling small amounts, then withdrawing. Winnings are documented as gambling income. Particularly effective in unregulated jurisdictions.

DETECTION: LOW
// 04 — FORENSIC DETECTION

How investigators
follow the money

Blockchain analytics firms like Chainalysis and Elliptic use multiple techniques to pierce through obfuscation layers.

🔍
Cluster Analysis

Blockchain analytics algorithms group addresses that are controlled by the same entity using heuristics like common-input-ownership and change address detection.

✓ Common-input ownership heuristic
✓ Change address fingerprinting
✓ Timing correlation attacks
✓ Dust attacks for address revelation
📊
Transaction Graph Analysis

Building a directed graph of all transactions. Suspicious patterns emerge: peel chains, fan-out then fan-in structures, and unusually complex routing through many wallets.

✓ Peel chain detection
✓ Fan-out / fan-in structuring patterns
✓ Velocity and volume anomalies
✓ Dormant wallet activation signals
🤖
Machine Learning Models

Supervised ML models trained on known criminal wallets can score new addresses for risk. Features include transaction patterns, counterparty exposure, and timing.

✓ Risk scoring from VASP exposure
✓ Anomaly detection on behavioral features
✓ Mixer interaction flags
✓ Darknet market address matching
🌐
OSINT + KYC Correlation

When funds eventually touch a regulated exchange (cashing out), KYC data becomes available to law enforcement via subpoena. The blockchain record is permanent and retroactive.

✓ Exchange subpoena for KYC data
✓ IP address logging at exchanges
✓ Cross-platform OSINT correlation
✓ Blockchain is immutable — permanent trail
// 05 — CASE HISTORY

Notable cases &
regulatory milestones

Real-world precedents that shaped crypto AML enforcement.

2013
Silk Road Seizure

FBI seized 144,000 BTC from Ross Ulbricht. First major case proving blockchain is a permanent ledger, not an untraceable system — investigators followed 5 years of transactions.

2016
Bitfinex Hack ($4.5B)

Hackers stole 119,754 BTC. Despite 5 years of layering attempts across thousands of wallets, blockchain analytics led to arrests in 2022 — demonstrating the durability of on-chain evidence.

2020
FinCEN Crypto Guidance

U.S. issued formal AML guidance requiring crypto exchanges to collect KYC for transactions over $3,000 and file SARs for suspicious activity, aligning crypto with banking regulations.

2022
Tornado Cash OFAC Sanctions

U.S. Treasury sanctioned Tornado Cash for laundering $7B+ including $455M from Lazarus Group (North Korea). First-ever sanctions on a smart contract, not an individual or company.

2023
Binance AML Settlement

Binance paid $4.3B — the largest corporate criminal settlement in U.S. history — for AML failures that allowed laundering by ransomware groups, darknet markets, and sanctioned entities.

2024
MiCA Regulation (EU)

Europe's Markets in Crypto-Assets regulation fully enacted, requiring all crypto VASPs to conduct KYC on transfers above €1,000 and report suspicious transactions — creating an EU-wide forensic framework.
