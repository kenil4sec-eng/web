// attack timeline
November 2021
Sky Mavis granted emergency DAO access
Due to high transaction volume, Sky Mavis was allowed to use Axie DAO's validator node. This was supposed to be temporary — but the permission was never revoked.
Late 2021 — Early 2022
Spear-phishing campaign targets Sky Mavis engineer
Lazarus Group (linked to North Korea) created a fake LinkedIn job offer. An engineer opened a malicious PDF, compromising their device and leaking 4 Sky Mavis validator private keys.
March 23, 2022 — 06:24 UTC
Transaction 1: 173,600 ETH withdrawn
Attacker submits withdrawal with 5 valid signatures. Contract verifies, finds quorum met (5 ≥ 5), releases 173,600 ETH (~$594M at the time).
March 23, 2022 — 06:26 UTC
Transaction 2: 25.5M USDC withdrawn
A second withdrawal transaction drains the USDC reserves. Both transactions are publicly visible on-chain. Nobody notices for 6 days.
March 29, 2022
Breach discovered — a user reports failed withdrawal
A user attempting to withdraw 5,000 ETH reports that the bridge has insufficient funds. Sky Mavis investigates and discovers the vault was drained 6 days prior.
April 2022 — onwards
FBI attributes hack to Lazarus Group (DPRK)
The US Treasury sanctioned the attacker's Ethereum address. Sky Mavis raised $150M to reimburse users. The bridge was relaunched with HSM key storage and 12-of-17 validator threshold.

// attack timeline
Early January 2022
Bug introduced in Wormhole Solana program upgrade
A code change introduced a missing account owner verification in the verify_signatures() function. The patch was committed to the public GitHub repo but not yet deployed to mainnet.
February 1, 2022 — ~20:00 UTC
Attacker reads the undeployed patch on GitHub
The fix was visible in the public repository before deployment. The attacker read the patch, understood the exact vulnerability it was addressing, and began constructing the exploit before the fix went live.
February 2, 2022 — 18:24 UTC
Exploit deployed: fake sysvar account created
Attacker creates an account they control, populates it with structurally valid (but fake) Guardian signature data, and calls verify_signatures() passing the fake account as the sysvar.
February 2, 2022 — 18:28 UTC
120,000 whETH minted on Solana
The contract accepts the fake VAA as valid and mints 120,000 wrapped ETH (~$320M) directly to the attacker's wallet. No ETH was ever deposited. The signature check was bypassed entirely.
February 2, 2022 — hours later
120,000 whETH bridged back to Ethereum for real ETH
The attacker redeems the counterfeit whETH through the bridge, receiving 120,000 real ETH from the Wormhole Ethereum vault — funds that real users had deposited as collateral.
February 3, 2022
Jump Crypto replenishes 120,000 ETH — protocol survives
Wormhole's backer Jump Crypto deposits 120,000 ETH to restore the 1:1 backing ratio and prevent whETH holders on Solana from losing their funds. The one-line fix is deployed. Bridge reopens.

// defenses and mitigations
Key management
Hardware security modules
Store validator keys in HSMs or air-gapped machines. Private keys should never touch internet-connected systems. Prevents spear-phishing from being sufficient alone.
Monitoring
Real-time withdrawal monitoring
Alert on large withdrawals. Circuit breakers that pause the bridge above threshold amounts. Ronin's $625M sat undetected for 6 days because no one was watching on-chain events.
Access control
Regular permission audits
Emergency access should be time-locked and automatically expire. Validator sets must be audited regularly. Never grant "temporary" permissions without a hard revocation date.
Verification
Account ownership checks
On Solana (and similar VMs), always verify that system accounts are owned by the expected program — not just that they have the right address format. The Wormhole fix was literally one check.
Architecture
Light client / ZK bridges
Replace trusted validators with cryptographic proofs. IBC-style light clients and ZK proof bridges eliminate the trusted-party assumption entirely — at the cost of complexity and latency.
Deployment
Private patch deployment
Never push security patches to a public repo before deployment. Wormhole's attacker read the undeployed fix on GitHub and exploited the bug before the patch went live — a critical ops failure.
